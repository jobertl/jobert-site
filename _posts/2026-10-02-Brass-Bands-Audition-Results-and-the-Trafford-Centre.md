# Brass Bands, Audition Results, and the Trafford Centre

**TL;DR:**
- I went to my first brass band concert
- I learned how I did on my auditions to date
- I went to Manchester's largest mall

![Collage for 2026-10-02](/assets/images/2026-10-02.JPG)

## Life Update

After going to a Medieval Faire being hosted in Manchester's City Centre on the **26th**, I went to a pub crawl hosted by the RNCMSU. Funnily enough, the group I was with for the event ditched it midway in favour of going to a nightclub instead, though I declined to join them due to feeling a bit fatigued.

On the **27th**, I attended my first brass band concert, which was put on by the KNDS Fairey Band at the University of Manchester in celebration of British composer Philip Sparke's 75th birthday. As such, the entire programme solely consisted of his music, though I have to admit that this was my first time hearing all of these pieces. This didn't stop me from thoroughly enjoying them, especially since Sparke's compositional style really shone through the chosen repertoire (which he personally helped to cultivate). However, with the hall the concert was performed in being rather small, such heavy brass instrumentation being blared at full strength inside it sounded a bit overwhelming. Now I really get why quite a lot of brass band shows are performed outdoors.

My second audition at the RNCM was for the BBC Philharmonic Professional Experience Scheme (similar to an unpaid internship for musicians) on the **28th**. Unfortunately, mainly due to being extremely busy on the weekend and rather out of form for the last couple of days, I wasn't able to make it past the first round, even though the material I played for the audition was condensed from the repertoire I had to prepare. This was also my first time playing with an RNCM accompanist, who rehearsed the assigned piece with me for all of ten minutes before the audition. My unfamiliarity with this method employed by the RNCM led me to be less prepared than I ought to be, but the audition and all the preparation I did for it was nevertheless an invaluable learning experience. At least the accompanist's services were free.

Shortly after the audition, I had my first studio lesson (yes, I'll be calling them studio lessons until the British term is stuck firmly in my head) with the rest of the RNCM trombones, where we played several trombone octet arrangements despite there being more than eight of us. Funnily enough, the arrangement of Nimrod from Elgar's Enigma Variations we played was by Maximilien Brisson, a Canadian sackbut player who I had met several times before. Once studio finished, me and several other members went to a nearby pub for a drink.

The **29th** brought more bad news, as I learned that I didn't make the cut for the Shostakovich 5 concert either. Despite these setbacks, both auditions were really valuable learning experiences for me, as I now know just how far the road ahead of me is at the RNCM. Still, I've got two whole years to improve myself while I'm here, and there'll be plenty of auditions down the line for me to succeed at.

Auditions weren't the only place where my luck ran out that day, as while I finally got a bass sackbut, it was after my first sackbut ensemble class due to scheduling, so I had to play on my modern trombone during said class. To make matters worse, it didn't come with a mouthpiece, and I don't have a small shank mouthpiece to use for now. At least I'm finally back in the early music business!

Since I had plenty of free time on the **30th**, I went to the Trafford Centre (think the Gold Coast but way bigger and indoors for Hong Kong-based readers and Metrotown but fancier for Vancouver-based readers) and had a pretty decent brunch of three small plates at the Mardi Gras-themed Wetherspoons there (yes, I know the reputation 'Spoons has in the UK, but it was my first time at one, so cut me some slack) before continuing to explore it. Once I had my fill of this mostly antiquity-themed shopping centre, I headed to the nearby Costco. Unfortunately, I didn't try the hotdog there, but I ate a beef chili jacket potato with cheese for dinner instead.

(Fun fact: The Trafford Centre is owned by the Canadian Pension Plan Investment Board.)

**October 1st** was the day of the new student wind orchestra rehearsal, which is apparently an RNCM tradition designed to acclimate new students to how rehearsals are run here, me included. Still, aside from having to arrive 10 minutes prior to the stated rehearsal time (to ensure enough time to set up and warm up), it essentially functioned the same as rehearsals back at UBC. One thing that stood out to me was the lack of iPads and foot pedal page turners being used, most likely due to the UK's stricter rules on copying music I mentioned last week.

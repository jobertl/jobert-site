## Special Update

Welcome to the first Special Update! As you may have guessed from the title and irregular posting time of this post, I’ll be making these updates whenever something extraordinary happens. In this case, it’s because I headed to Birmingham on **October 24th and 25th** for the annual British Trombone Festival.

Ironically enough, I almost couldn’t make it to the festival because my train to Birmingham was cancelled on the morning of my departure. The one thing that saved me from having to spend money on another train or bus ticket last-minute was a new British government policy enacted just last month, which stated that ticketholders can ride any alternative train to their destination in the two hours before or after their original train if it was cancelled. Thanks to this, I was able to make it to Birmingham on time, albeit with an added transfer (my original train was direct).

After I got to Birmingham and registered my attendance for the festival, my first order of business for the festival was doing some quick warmups with the other attendees, before sitting in on a theatre playing masterclass with Christopher Augustine, the trombonist for the West End’s production of Wicked (which I incidentally watched a couple of years ago). I then listened to a jazz performance by the British Army’s Household Division Trombone Ensemble, and had a short lunch break in time for a breathing seminar hosted by a certain RNCM trombone tutor, which I found surprisingly useful in visualising how I breathe when playing.

Unfortunately, I wasn’t able to attend a couple of events later on due to some personal business I urgently needed to deal with, but I managed to make it in time for the massed trombone choir rehearsal for the second day’s gala performance finale. That was soon followed by a panel discussion with some of the festival’s presenters and guests about all aspects of trombone playing, and the first day concluded with a double jazz act of Elliot Mason and Rory Ingram’s Trombone Assembly, two show-stopping displays of improvisational mastery that may very well have made me want to try out some jazz myself.

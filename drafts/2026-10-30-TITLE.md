## Life Update

### Music Things

Even though I mainly spent it playing excerpts and pieces I had already done before, I was still pushed to my limits during my first lesson this week. This was primarily due to being challenged to take less breaths when playing, which quickly left me gasping for air, although it also allowed me to convey my musical ideas more smoothly. Since I don't leave myself in such a state that often, which would certainly help me develop my stamina and air capacity, this experience made me wonder if I could incorporate something along these lines into my practice sessions to further test and improve myself.

This week's studio class was a classic 'play-to-the-class' session with an RNCM twist: we were provided with a college accompanist. While I had a couple other potential pieces in mind for this class, I ultimately decided to play the first movement of Gordon Jacob's trombone concerto, since I believed its mixture of lyrical and technical sections would provide me with the greatest amount of feedback. This being a last-minute change meant I had less time to brush up on the piece than intended, so my resulting performance was noticeably worse than in my recital or my Guildhall audition video, but I did receive some useful pointers on my playing style in the end. Ironically enough, this year's Guildhall auditions were also the reason one of my fellow trombones played this piece for the session as well, and certainly sounded better than my rusty rendition.

Having obtained a small shank trombone mouthpiece a few weeks back, my sackbut lessons could now proceed as planned, though I have yet to fully adapt to my new bass sackbut being pitched in F rather than Eb like my previous one. This naturally resulted in my existing muscle memory working against me whenever I tried to sight read, which was an issue that persisted into sackbut ensemble rehearsal later in the day. However, that was only the second-most pressing issue during rehearsal, as we discovered that the score and the parts we were reading had different metres and barlines, despite being published by the same company! While we still managed to play something coherent by the end, I felt that trying to work around this engraving error made this particular rehearsal less productive than it should have been.

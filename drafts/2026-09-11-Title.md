## Life Update

While walking through Hong Kong Island on the **6th**, I came across a tree planted by John Denver back in 1994. Since I had no idea that it existed up to this point, the tree was honestly a pretty cool find. It just goes to show how much there is to discover throughout Hong Kong’s urban fabric, even for a local like me.

During a shopping trip on the **9th**, I came across a rather strange product: a bottle of Hong Kong-style milk tea produced by a Japanese company for Japanese consumers, now being sold in Hong Kong. With actual Hong Kong milk tea literally available at most restaurants in the area, and being sold for way less than the Japanese version, you have to wonder who the target audience for this milk tea is in Hong Kong. Maybe curious people like me? (Common sense ultimately prevailed and I did not buy it.)

## Baked Pork Chop Rice Review

This week, I’ll be reviewing Sheung Bun’s take on the dish. While it’s one of the places I live close to, I mainly go there for dinner, so this is the first time in a while I’ve had their baked pork chop rice.

Sauce: 2/5 - Rather flavourless, tasted a touch salty more than anything else.

Rice: 5/5 - Excellent base of fried rice. Strikes the perfect balance between being mushy and grainy.

Pork chop: 3/5 - Aside from it being sliced, nothing much to write home about, although I did need to cut some of my slices further.

Additional toppings: 2/5 - At least I can see the fresh tomatoes in it.

Portion size: 5/5 - Pretty good value for money, especially if you go during teatime like I did.

Total score and final remarks: 17/25 - Considering that Sheung Bun’s main specialties are its dinner offering such as chicken and fried oysters, their baked pork chop rice certainly punches above its expected weight, especially for its teatime price. However, I may be being a bit charitable with my rating here, seeing as it’s one of my frequent haunts.

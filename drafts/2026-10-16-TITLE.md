## Life Update

On the music side of things, I unfortunately had my prior suspicions confirmed when I got an email saying that I didn't progress to the next round of the Hallé's Professional Experience Scheme. Since my audition was already in the past by then, I had time to get over it and pre-emptively accept my rejection, so actually receiving it didn't sting as much. Now that I know how far I still need to go during my time at the RNCM, I can continue down that path, and hopefully get better results in my other auditions here.

Having my first lesson with my other tutor this week was definitely a step in this direction, as we spent it working on some of my previous audition material. Seeing as I often gained the biggest breakthroughs in my playing whenever I had lessons with other professors at UBC (back when there was more than one trombone professor teaching there), I'm optimistic that this approach will continue to yield significant results for me at the RNCM.

This week's studio class focused on improving our ensemble/choir playing, particularly within quartets. Some of the material we looked at included Bach's chorales, along with Beethoven and Bruckner's equali for trombones. I was honestly impressed by how impressive some groupings sounded, especially considering they spent precisely zero time rehearsing together beforehand, though I'll have to admit that I did have some oversights when it came to my turn to play.

On the non-music side of things, I visited the Manchester IKEA to buy some household essentials, while also getting to enjoy a discounted fish and chips lunch courtesy of the restaurant there. Strangely enough, this was my first time eating fish and chips in Manchester (though not in the UK, given that I've visited London before), and even though it isn't the best rendition of the dish I've ever had, it's certainly got value for money.

Another notable lunch I had was with an old friend of mine who I've known since my days in Hong Kong, where we swapped stories about what we had been up to since we last saw each other. In true English fashion, we enjoyed it at a pub, but the best thing about this locale was that it was on a hill overlooking Manchester. Combined with the excellent weather, quite the rarity in Britain, the sight of sheep and horses grazing in the nearby pastures made this idyllic sight one I'll never forget. (Ignoring the fact that I had a tasty dinner of lamb shank just the night before.)

## Life Update

I went to the Hong Kong Heritage Museum’s Leslie Cheung 70th birthday exhibit on the **13th**. For those of you who don’t know, Leslie Cheung was a renowned Hong Kong singer-actor who was most active during the 1980s and 1990s, with a globe-spanning fanbase amongst Chinese-speaking communities. Despite his suicide in 2003, his music and movies are still remembered fondly by scores of Hongkongers and other admirers worldwide, some of whom lent their prized memorabilia to the Heritage Museum for this milestone exhibit.

Unfortunately, I was not one of the Hongkongers who got to experience Cheung’s star power first hand, as he died shortly after I was born, but I have listened to some of his music and watched several movies he starred in growing up. That said, I felt weirdly emotional while walking through the exhibit and learning more about who Cheung was, almost as if I was hearing stories about a beloved relative who I never had the chance to meet in person. If I had to put how I felt into a few words, I’d say it was like experiencing a fragment of the Hong Kong that existed before my time, distilled into the life of Leslie Cheung.

(For those of you in Vancouver, here’s a little fun fact for you. Cheung lived in Vancouver during his hiatus from showbiz, and there’s a bench dedicated to his memory in Stanley Park. It’s in front of the teahouse looking out into Burrard Inlet.)

## Life Update

### Music Things

Unfortunately, my prior suspicions were confirmed when I got an email saying that I didn't progress to the next round of the Hallé's Professional Experience Scheme. Since my audition was already in the past by then, I had time to get over it and pre-emptively accept my rejection, so actually receiving it didn't sting as much. Now that I know how far I still need to go during my time at the RNCM, I can continue down that path, and hopefully get better results in my other auditions here.

Having my first lesson with my other tutor this week was definitely a step in this direction, as we spent it working on some of my previous audition material. Seeing as I often gained the biggest breakthroughs in my playing whenever I had lessons with other professors at UBC (back when there was more than one trombone professor teaching there), I'm optimistic that this approach will continue to yield significant results for me at the RNCM.

Strangely, my other private lesson this week also fit this mould, despite me spending almost all of it just playing four notes. With more than a few people telling me that my fundamentals needed significant work, playing long notes to ingrain how an 'ideal note' should sound in my mind is definitely one way to make improvements, although I'll need to spend a lot of time in the practice room for this to yield proper results.

This week's studio class focused on improving our ensemble/choir playing, particularly within quartets. Some of the material we looked at included Bach's chorales, along with Beethoven and Bruckner's equali for trombones. I was honestly impressed by how impressive some groupings sounded, especially considering they spent precisely zero time rehearsing together beforehand, though I'll have to admit that I did have some oversights when it was my turn to play.

I also tried out for a non-RNCM orchestra after answering their call for trombonists. With their previous repertoire being rather eclectic and right up my alley (including the much-underplayed Mendelssohn 5), I was interested enough to travel quite a distance from the RNCM to participate. Even though I found this first rehearsal quite underwhelming, especially since a number of players were ill or recovering, the programme for their upcoming concert was intriguing enough to convince me to come back in the future.

### Music-Related Things

I had my first curry night with the other RNCM trombones. Or rather, a drinking night with a side of curry, seeing as we visited three pubs before and after said curry. My first drink of the night was a cider at a pub close to the RNCM we met at, before heading to the curry house. However, since the curry house was a fair distance away on foot, we stopped at another pub on the way to enjoy another drink, in my case a salted caramel ale. When it came time for the curry, which we had at a place I ate at before, I went for lamb and chickpeas, before finishing my night at a third pub with another ale. Aside from a certain party earlier this year, it's almost safe to say that this was the most drinks I've ever had.

I also had a job interview for a potential position within the RNCM. Since I'm allowed to work a certain number of hours as an international student, having a bit of extra income from this job would be nice. Unfortunately, I soon learned that I did not get accepted, so it's back to the drawing board for more ideas to earn some extra cash (legally, of course). The interview was still a good learning experience, especially since it was the first one I did in-person, and (in my opinion) more thorough than the one I did for my previous job.

### Non-Music Things

I visited the Manchester IKEA to buy some household essentials, while also getting to enjoy a discounted fish and chips lunch courtesy of the restaurant there. Strangely enough, this was my first time eating fish and chips in Manchester (though not in the UK, given that I've visited London before), and even though it isn't the best rendition of the dish I've ever had, it's certainly got value for money.

Another notable lunch I had was with an old friend of mine who I've known since my days in Hong Kong, where we swapped stories about what we had been up to since we last saw each other. In true English fashion, we enjoyed it at a pub, but the best thing about this locale was that it was on a hill overlooking Manchester. Combined with the excellent weather, quite the rarity in Britain, the sight of sheep and horses grazing in the nearby pastures made this idyllic sight one I'll never forget. (Ignoring the fact that I had a tasty dinner of lamb shank just the night before.)

## Reflections

Even after gaining an offer to study at the Royal Northern College of Music, I sometimes still ask myself why I chose to go down such a challenging path, moving across continents yet again to pursue further studies. The short answer is that I wanted to expand my horizons and see how far I could go as a performing musician, hopefully realizing my ambitions along the way. The long answer is... a bit more complicated.

As I mentioned in a previous post, I never intended to be a music student, much less go down the path of a performer. Instead, the career I dedicated myself to pursuing for most of high school was engineering, since it appeared to offer better job prospects at the time (mind you, this was before the rise of generative AI). That didn't mean I would give up music entirely; it would just be something I'd pursue as a simple hobby. While I did initially start university as an engineering student, and even got good grades, I quickly discovered that it wasn't for me. Long story short, I then transferred schools and programmes, and the rest is history.


One of the first steps of this process involved choosing whether I would be a performance, education, or general studies major. Since the requirements for being a performance major were the most demanding, I put it as my first choice (and general studies as my second), reasoning that it would be easier to switch out than in from this position if I found it not to my liking. In a way, this sums up my reason for aiming high: if it pans out, I'd be one step closer to achieving my ambitions; if not, I'd at least get an idea of the level I was at, and either focus on improving myself or pivot to other goals entirely.

With music schools in the UK being some of the world's most renowned, I decided to shoot my shot and apply for a few, especially since my other main choice of US schools wasn't tenable for obvious reasons. Despite knowing that it would be a bit of a long shot, I decided to try anyways, and I now have undeniable proof of how far I've come as a musician. Still, I know this is just the beginning of my next journey, and a lot can change over the course of two years, but I now have a better idea of what I can do and how much I can still hope to achieve.

(Engineer) 

## Cinematheque

In this section, I'll be discussing films I watched lately (spoiler-free).

As teased last week, here's my thoughts on the film **Remarkably Bright Creatures**, directed by Olivia Newman and starring Sally Field, Lewis Pullman, and Alfred Molina. The film centres around Tova (Field), a woman near retirement who works as a small-town aquarium janitor trying to navigate life after the death of her son. When struggling wannabe musician Cameron (Pullman) ends up in town following an accident, the two of them begin gradually bonding over the loss of their family members, aided by the aquarium's octopus Marcellus (Molina).

Aside from being an excellent film overall, and almost certainly in the running for my favourite film of the year, one of the main reasons Remarkably Bright Creatures caught my attention is because it was mainly filmed in Deep Cove, a North Vancouver neighbourhood that quickly became one of my favourites to visit. While it is surprisingly well-connected to the rest of the Vancouver area by public transport, Deep Cove's small size and remote location within the Burrard Inlet gives it a cosy vibe, all with a great view of the mountains and sea that make Vancouver so spectacular to live in. For that reason, it's the perfect locale to set a feel-good film like Remarkably Bright Creatures.

Despite the film's main hook being Marcellus the octopus, an intelligent, snarky, and proud cephalopod who constantly points out the flaws in human behaviour through his deadpan narration, the plot of Remarkably Bright Creatures doesn't really revolve around or involve him aside from a couple pivotal moments. Instead, the film focuses entirely on the shifting dynamic between Tova and Cameron, who are already interesting enough from their circumstances as mentioned above, and how they help and support each otehr through challenging times in their lives.
